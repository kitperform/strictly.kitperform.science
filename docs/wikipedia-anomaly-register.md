# Wikipedia anomaly register

This register lists 257 format anomalies (A001–A257) found in the English Wikipedia pages for *Strictly Come Dancing* series 1 to 24. An anomaly is anything that departs from the plain pattern of "each couple dances once a week, four judges score out of 10, the lowest goes home on Sunday", or anything in the way the page encodes a fact that a naive parser would get wrong. Each entry records where it happens, what Wikipedia actually says or marks up, and what it means for the data model.

**How it was compiled.** Four research passes, each covering six series (1–6, 7–12, 13–18, 19–24), read the live wikitext of every series page on 8 November 2026 (series 24 was in progress, with week 7 the latest complete week) and cross-checked it against the HTML captures held in this repository under `data/wikipedia/series/0NN/`. Where the live page and the capture disagree, the entry says so. Identifiers were assigned in series order after the four passes were merged, so the id order is also roughly chronological. Each entry carries a confidence value: `read_from_page` means the fact was read directly from the live wikitext and, where the repo holds a capture, checked against it; `inferred` means the fact is deduced from the absence of something on the page rather than stated there; `memory` means the fact rests partly on the researcher's recollection and should be re-verified before it is relied on. Counts in this register: 255 `read_from_page`, 1 `inferred`, 1 `memory`.

**Why it matters.** The register is the acceptance test for the data model. The design rule for this project is that every historical corner case must be representable declaratively, as rows, enumeration values or rule-table entries, never as special-case code. A model passes when every anomaly below can be loaded without inventing a score, losing a fact or writing an `if series == 6` branch. The one-paragraph implication at the head of each category states what the model must provide; the per-anomaly implications give the detail. Wikipedia text quoted here is data about the source, not instruction.

The file is generated by the research pipeline script `gen_docs.py` from the phase-1 research JSON (`anomalies.json`); edit the JSON or the script, not this file.

### no_elimination

*Rounds, weeks and eliminations · 14 anomalies*

**Modelling implication.** A competition week does not always end with an elimination. Week 1 has had no elimination in every series (scores, and in some series votes, carried into week 2), the S6 semi-final had none after a tie at the top, and mid-series withdrawals (S21 W11, S23 W9) have cancelled an elimination and rolled the vote into the next week. The model therefore needs a results (voting) round that is distinct from both the episode and the week: a round references the set of weeks whose scores count towards it (1+2, 9+10, 11+12, 13+14), carries a has_elimination flag and a reason, and is the thing to which bottom-two and eliminated statuses attach. Wikipedia's combined columns are derived views of such a round and must never be stored as scores.

### combined_weeks_scores

*Rounds, weeks and eliminations · 2 anomalies*

**Modelling implication.** Where a vote spans two weeks Wikipedia inserts a synthetic column ('1+2', '13+14') and places the bottom-two and eliminated colouring there rather than in a real week. These columns are derived from the round definition: store the round (which weeks it aggregates) and recompute the totals, keeping the derived figures separate from the primary judge marks and tolerating the page later changing its mind about what counts.

### multiple_dances_per_week

*Rounds, weeks and eliminations · 10 anomalies*

**Modelling implication.** Quarter-finals, semi-finals and some split week-1 formats give each couple two or more scored routines in one week, and Wikipedia merges identical scores into a single rowspan cell. One row per Performance with its own judge marks is mandatory; the weekly total, the 'a+b=total' cell and the highest/lowest-of-the-night flags are aggregates over a couple's performances in that round. A couple may repeat a dance (re-choreographed routines, favourites), so Performance needs a repeat/role flag, and dance first-appearance queries (American Smooth S3, Argentine tango S4) fall out of the per-performance table joined to a dance lookup.
